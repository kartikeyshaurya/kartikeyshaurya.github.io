Agents & LLMs Creed Infotech

The agent that answers the phone

Voice-enabled AI agents now take operational calls at a freight company and finish the workflow themselves.

Logistics operations are conversations: a driver calling about a pickup, a customer asking where a load is, a broker confirming a rate. Each one used to land on a person. At ValueLoads, Kartikey designed and deployed voice-enabled AI agents that hold those conversations and act on them.

The agents listen, reason over the live call with a language model, and speak back, with the latency kept tight enough that it feels like a call rather than a form. Because they call the company's operational tools directly, a conversation ends with the record updated, not with a ticket for someone else to work.

The parts that mattered most were the unglamorous ones: a clean hand-off to a human when confidence drops, and observability on every turn so a bad call can be replayed and diagnosed instead of argued about.

Agents & LLMs Creed Infotech

Support that knows when not to answer

AI assistants for an online tutoring company and a legal-services firm, grounded in each business's own material.

Vnaya teaches children online. Virtue Legal Services advises clients on matters where a wrong answer has consequences. Both had support queues growing faster than their teams, and both needed an assistant that answered from what the company actually says rather than from what a model guesses.

Kartikey designed and deployed AI-powered support chatbots for each, retrieving from the company's own documents and policies so that replies are traceable. The deployments are separate but share tooling, so an improvement to one ships to both.

For the legal assistant the key feature is restraint: it knows which questions it must decline and route to a person. Evaluation sets were built from real tickets before launch, so the first users were not the test set.

Agents & LLMs Primotech

Agents that collect data and hand work to each other

A multi-agent platform built on LangChain and LangGraph, with fine-tuned Pegasus and BERT and recommendations served from Elasticsearch.

On the Rejara platform Kartikey architected and deployed LLM-driven agents that gather data autonomously and collaborate: one collects, another validates, another condenses. Building them on LangGraph gave each agent explicit state, so long-running tasks can pause, resume and be inspected rather than restarted.

Underneath the agents sit models he fine-tuned for the job: Pegasus for conversational summarisation and BERT for topic classification. Their outputs feed Elasticsearch-based recommendation pipelines that reduced manual review time while improving the accuracy of the insights reviewers saw.

Each agent runs as its own microservice with its own scaling profile, orchestrated as workflows, which is what let the platform adapt across user segments without rewrites.

Data & Pipelines Sapio Analytics

A 160 GB data lake stops being the bottleneck

Neural Ocean, the company's internal data lake, was rebuilt as a warehouse on Airflow. Writes got 40–50% faster and storage shrank by a fifth.

Sapio's analysts worked on top of a 160 GB+ internal data lake that was slow to write to and expensive to keep. Kartikey implemented a warehouse architecture with Airflow: raw, cleaned and modelled layers, each hop owned by a DAG, so lineage is visible and reruns are safe.

Partitioning and compaction were tuned to the queries people actually ran, which is where the gains came from: write throughput up 40–50%, storage footprint down 20%, response times 10–20% faster across the board and analytical workloads up to 40% faster.

In the same role he built a PII detection pipeline, so datasets could be shared internally without a manual review step, and profiling tooling that the analytics team used daily.

Public Sector Sapio Analytics

Drivers earn more, and stay

A regression-driven clustering model matched incentives to how drivers actually worked. Income rose 8–10%; attrition fell about 15%.

A driver workforce is not one population. Kartikey built regression-driven performance clustering that separated drivers by how they actually drove and earned, so that incentives and routing could be set per group instead of for an average that fit nobody.

The measured result was an 8–10% rise in average driver income and a roughly 15% drop in attrition, the number the business cared about most.

Public Sector Government initiative

Third place for an anonymiser that hides names and faces

Fine-tuned DistilBERT finds personal information in text; YuNet finds faces in images; a UI lets each team set its own redaction rules.

Data is useful to share and dangerous to share. For the West Bengal Government's data-security initiative Kartikey built Data Anonymizer, which placed third.

Text passes through a DistilBERT model fine-tuned for PII detection; images pass through YuNet for face detection and anonymisation. Because every organisation has its own idea of what counts as sensitive, the tool ships with a UI for customisable redaction rules rather than a fixed list.
